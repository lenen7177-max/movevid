AI Celebrity Autograph turns one photo into a fan-cam clip of you as the star of a fan meet: a giant poster of your face, fans filming on their phones, and an autograph for one of them. This guide covers what the template does, which photo to pick and how to get the video onto TikTok.

## What the autograph trend is

The autograph trend takes an ordinary photo and makes it look like someone filmed you being famous. You walk up to a signing table under a giant poster of your own face, the fans lift their phones, and you sign a photo for one of them. The joke lands because it looks like real fan footage of a celebrity, and the celebrity is you. No real celebrity appears in the clip: the fans and the bodyguard are generic extras.

## Photo tips

- **One person, waist-up or full body.** The photo plays the star, so a photo of just you works best.
- **A clear, front-facing face.** Faces turned away or covered by hair, sunglasses or a hand come out less like you.
- **Even light.** Soft daylight works better than harsh shadows or strong backlight.
- **JPG, PNG or WebP, under 20 MB.** Larger, sharper photos keep more detail.
- **Only people who agreed.** Use your own photo or a friend's who said yes. Photos of celebrities or public figures don't pass the photo review.

## Common mistakes

- **Group photos.** Several people in one photo make it unclear who the star is. Crop the photo to one person.
- **Using a celebrity's photo.** The point is that you are the star. Photos of real celebrities are rejected by the review.
- **Posting it as real.** Keep TikTok's AI-generated label on and don't present the clip as a real meeting with anyone famous.

**Is the autograph a real signature?**
