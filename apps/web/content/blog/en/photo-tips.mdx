The template decides the moves, the camera and the music. Your photos decide who's in the video — so a good photo makes the biggest difference. Here's what works best.

## 1. One person per photo

Each photo slot plays one person in the scene. Group shots make it hard for the model to know who you mean, so crop to a single person.

## 2. A clear, front-facing face

Faces turned to the side, hidden by hair, sunglasses or a hand often come out less like the person. A straight-on or slightly angled face works best.

## 3. Even light

Soft daylight beats harsh shadows. Avoid strong backlight that leaves the face dark.

## 4. Enough resolution

Use photos at least 300 px on each side — larger is better. Very small or heavily compressed images lose detail.

## 5. Only people who agreed to appear

Use photos of yourself or friends who are happy to star in the video. Photos of public figures are rejected by the model's review.
